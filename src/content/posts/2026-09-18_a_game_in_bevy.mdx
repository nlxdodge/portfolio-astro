I have signed up for the Fragments of Infinity Game Jam from my company that I work for and wanted to partake with Rust & Bevy. To see how far I can go with the new engine on the block. The Game Jam had different themes on how to score points.

I made an initial draft of what I wanted to build in two to three days. And had a solid gameplay loop for re-playability. You basically have to escape from the ever growing abyss which is growlingly flooding into the drilling space, you are a dwarf working on a drill platform in the middle of the ocean. But something happend and angered the Abyss.

I have some intrest in Rust and found out that there was already a game engine made, called [Bevy](https://bevy.org/). So, I choose to pick this engine and deep dive into this in the two weeks that I got.

I checked the initial documentation and made a schaffolding to quickly start out with the project. I first invisioned to make this completly in 3D. I downloaded [Blender](https://www.blender.org/) and watch a video to get started on moddeling. But after the midway check-in for the Game Jam I found out it was going to take to much time.

I then switched to 2D and found some nice tools to work with to generate art and help my workflow.

- Free* [OpenCode.ai](https://opencode.ai/download) (AI via the terminal) which I used in conjunction to learn and on the final days to complete they games. I was very impressed of how hands of the models that they have can be. Which can be found at [Models.dev](https://models.dev/models) and use this model only on the Medium setting [Muse Spark 1.3](https://models.dev/models/meta/muse-spark-1.3/).
- Free* [AI text to image generator](https://perchance.org/ai-text-to-image-generator) to generate proof of concept art, which I used as final art because of time constraints.
- Free* [Background remover (local)](https://resizeimage.io/background-remover) which runs with JavaScript/WebAssembly right on your own machine, so this takes away some manual work trying to make the generated images background free.

* (It could be that in time the tools are not existing anymore, or they are now paid only).

When making the game I needed to use some things like physics, particles, logging, making the level, debugging, packaging and more.

I first checked out the Physics engines and landed on [Avian2D](https://github.com/avianphysics/avian). Where I used the 3D version at first, but the 2D one also works great. I had to get up to speed with how to do the Physics but from the examples from TODO and TODO it was not to hard to figure out.

But before doing that I had to get the Bevy Basics under my belt. I had to found out what an (the Bevy also uses this) [ECS system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entity_component_system) was, as I never heard of this concept before. And Bevy exclusively used this, I used [this](https://bevy.org/learn/quick-start/getting-started/ecs/) to learn as well how it's being implemented in Rust. Although the Query and Single useage was still a bit alienating, it's fine now.  

After learning the basics from the Bevy website I found some others with helpful documentation and examples.
